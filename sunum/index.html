The plan

1Simulation

1.1

Create simulation environments

Real homes rebuilt from listing photos, with doors, drawers and appliances that work.

1.2

Create simulated humans

Residents with goals, routines and habits, living in those homes day after day.

1.3

Train and integrate intention models

Models that read what a person is about to do, so the robot can plan around it.

1.4

Test anticipation on real video

Predict each person’s next action and the task it serves from the video so far.

2Real world

2.1

Short-term adaptation

In a lab home with real people, the robot keeps up as the day’s plans change.

2.2

Long-term adaptation

Over weeks, it learns a household’s habits and follows them as they shift.

Data and sources

Floor area. The project reports 35,000 listings collected. The archived catalogue from 10 November 2026 contains 8,557 detailed records from İstanbul, Ankara and İzmir. Of its 2,663 flats advertised as 3+1 (three bedrooms and one living room), 2,641 report a positive net floor area. Their median is 115 m². The median advertised gross area is 130 m² across the 2,596 flats reporting it. Each source listing is counted once. Different listings can describe the same dwelling.

Why Türkiye. People per home and people living alone are the figures of the comparison tab, with its sources. People aged 65 or over were 10.6% of the population in 2024, 9.1 million people, and TÜİK’s main projection is 13.5% for 2030 (Elderly Statistics 2024). About 6.7 million households included someone aged 65 or over in 2024 (Statistics on Families 2024). Source: TÜİK Elderly Statistics 2024 (9.11 million people aged 65+; 10.6%, projected 13.5% in 2030) and Statistics on Families 2024 (6.73 million households). What is learned in simulation holds only as far as the simulated homes and households resemble the real ones. A study from elsewhere shows how much the setting matters to such systems: six object recognisers were 15 to 20 points more accurate on household items photographed in the United States than in Somalia or Burkina Faso (DeVries and others, 2019; 264 homes in 54 countries). It was not measured on Turkish homes. Paper

What the listings count. These figures come from the 8,557 detailed records (İstanbul 3,202, İzmir 2,880, Ankara 2,475) and only from listings that state the feature. Kitchen type is stated in 7,122: 5,121 are closed and 2,001 open; closed kitchens are 82% of Ankara’s, 77% of İstanbul’s and 58% of İzmir’s. A balcony is stated in 7,917: 6,820 have one, 27 more a French balcony. The number of rooms is stated in 8,372: 7,997 are written as rooms plus salons, 7,549 of them with one salon. A listing shows that a home has the feature. How a household lives in it is custom, and the listings give no count for it: shoes and slippers at the door, guests who come without notice, the salon kept for them, tea on a tray, carpets on the floors, cooking with the kitchen door shut, laundry and summer meals on the balcony, and meals before dawn and after sunset in Ramadan.

Simulation. The floor plan illustrates possible household choices. Listing photographs document visible space and objects. An entry pause, a guest’s seat, an open door or the use of a rack is a setting to vary in the simulation.

Beyond Türkiye. Türkiye is where ADA starts. The same steps can be repeated wherever homes are advertised with photographs: rebuild the homes from the listings, count what the listings state, and add the customs of the place as settings to vary. The comparison shows how much households and homes differ between cities. Only Turkish homes have been built so far.

Comparison. Türkiye’s household figures are national; the floor area refers to the three-city 3+1 sample. City figures use local housing stocks and years. New York’s 56 m² refers to rentals.     1.4

    How early can ADA read a person’s goal?

    • Real recordings

    • LEMMA

      People at home, alone or in pairs, each given a task.

    • HiK

      People using real kitchens, recorded as moving bodies.

    • DARai

      50 people at daily activities, with goals marked at three levels.

    • MultiSensor-Home

      Two real homes, each watched by five fixed wall cameras.

    • Simulated homes

    • Online Watch-And-Help

      A helper joins a person on a household task in a simulated home.

    • Habitat 3.0

      A robot and a person tidy a simulated home together.

    • Scored from wall cameras and the robot’s camera

      Each simulated test runs twice: as published, then from these cameras alone.

    Does simulation training help?

    Test on people the model has never seen. Compare simulation alone, real recordings alone and both together, with the same amount of real labels for the last two.

    Datasets and test details

    LEMMA. The task each person was given is the goal label. All four published scores are reported: one or two people, one or two tasks. ADA is scored on the same rows, from the room camera alone and with the head cameras added. Dataset request · Paper

    HiK. 3D body poses of people using four real kitchens. Scored on 2 s pelvis path error (mm) beside OCSD and HUMOF, plus fixture-use and departure anticipation. Dataset

    DARai. 50 people, fixed cameras in each room, goals marked at three levels. Scored on the authors’ anticipation measure from the fixed cameras. Dataset

    MultiSensor-Home. Two real smart homes, each watched by five fixed wall cameras across daily household activities. Scored on class mAP within Home 1 and across held-out homes beside MultiTSF. Paper

    Online Watch-And-Help. A helper works out what a person in a simulated home is doing and helps. Scored on how much sooner the person finishes. As published the helper is handed the state of the home. The second run gives it cameras alone. Code · Paper

    Habitat 3.0, Social Rearrangement. A robot and a person tidy a simulated home together. The robot knows which objects go where and has to work around what the person does. Scored on time saved against the person alone, and on collisions. Paper

    Rules. Whole rooms — and whole people wherever the published split allows — stay out of training. Thresholds are set on separate development splits, and a named comparator is scored beside every result.

    What the two cameras see

    One robot, three rounds

    The same robot and the same 15 things in every round, and in every round the participant can simply tell it what to do. What changes is what the robot does when nobody has told it anything. With ADA’s model it works around what the participant is doing and about to do. In another round it keeps to a fixed order, and in another it reacts only to the person’s current movement without forecasting goals. Which round comes first rotates across participants.

    What is scored

    After each round the participant rates how natural working with the robot felt. The recordings give how often the two got in each other’s way, how long either waited, and how often the participant had to tell the robot what to do or correct it. Drawn to show the plan, before any measurement.

    What was corrected

    The jug, moved to the other end of the table

    What was said

    “The round tray next time”

    How the round felt

    The participant’s rating after each round

    Learning in context

    The same participant comes back for each visit. Between visits the model stays as it is, and its prior information on this participant grows with what was done, corrected and said. On the next visit the robot starts from there. In a five-day desk study, a routine memory recovered from a changed routine after a single update.

    What is scored

    Visit by visit: how many of the 15 things the robot gets right without being told, how often the participant has to say the same thing twice, and how natural the participant rates each round. Each visit also has a round with the general prior information, for comparison. Drawn to show the plan, before any measurement.
